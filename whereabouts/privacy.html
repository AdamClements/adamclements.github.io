Whereabouts: privacy policy

Last updated 5 November 2026.

Whereabouts (the Android app uk.adamclements.whereabouts) records where you go and how you get there, all day, and shows each day as a timeline. This page says what it reads, what it keeps and the one thing it can send, only if you turn it on.

Your days stay on your phone

  • Location, including in the background. Whereabouts collects your location (GPS while you're moving, the phone's network location while you're still) even when the app is closed or not in use. That's how it records the places you go and the journeys between them without you pressing anything.
  • Steps. With the Physical activity permission it reads the phone's step counter, to tell walking from running and to notice when you set off.
  • Your photos (optional). If you allow it, it reads when your photos were taken, to put them on your days, and shows small thumbnails of them. It only reads them and never changes them. A small copy of one of your photos may be kept as a place's picture. On Android 14 and later you can allow just some photos.
  • Place names (cafés, pubs, shops, stations and villages in Great Britain, from OpenStreetMap and Overture Maps) come with the app and are looked up on the phone.
  • All of this, and the names and notes you add, is kept only in the app's own storage on your phone. It is never sold, never used for adverts and never sent to us: Whereabouts has no server.
  • You can pause recording until the next morning, or turn it off until you turn it back on (Settings, Recording). While paused or off, nothing is recorded.
  • It is left out of Android's cloud backup. Android's phone-to-phone transfer, when you move to a new phone, can copy it across. Settings has Forget a day and Forget everything; uninstalling the app or clearing its data removes it all.
  • Versions before 0.3.1 also listened for nearby Bluetooth devices, to show who you were with. That feature has gone and nothing is scanned now; what was recorded then stays on the phone, unused, until you use Forget a day or Forget everything.

The one thing that can leave the phone, if you turn it on: pictures of places

This is off unless you turn it on (in set-up, in a place's "Photo for…" sheet, or in Settings, "Photos of places from the web"). With it off, nothing leaves the phone at all.

With it on, when you've named a place and none of your photos from there can stand for it, Whereabouts asks Wikimedia for a picture of it (when it's named, and again later if none was found). It sends the place's name and its position, rounded to about 100 metres, to Wikidata's query service (query.wikidata.org) and the Wikimedia Commons API (commons.wikimedia.org), over HTTPS, and fetches the picture from thumb.wikimedia.org or upload.wikimedia.org.

  • It never asks about your home, about Home or Work if you haven't given them a name, or about a place you've marked as someone's home. (A named Work place is looked up like any other.)
  • It sends no account, no ID and nothing else about you. Each request says it comes from Whereabouts and its version, with Whereabouts' contact address, as Wikimedia asks of apps. Like any request on the internet, Wikimedia sees your phone's IP address; its handling of that is covered by the Wikimedia Foundation's privacy policy.
  • The answer is kept on the phone; a place that had no picture isn't asked about again for 30 days.
  • A name the app only guessed is never sent: only names you've given.
  • You can turn it off again at any time: Settings, "Photos of places from the web".

What Whereabouts doesn't do

  • No adverts, no analytics, no crash reporting, no accounts, no sign-in.
  • Nothing is sold or shared with anyone, apart from the picture requests to Wikimedia above, if you turn them on.

Permissions

  • Location, all the time: to record your day in the background.
  • Physical activity: steps, to tell a walk from a run.
  • Photos (optional): to put your photos on your days. You can allow only some.
  • Notifications: the quiet notification Android needs while it records.
  • Run at start-up: so recording carries on after the phone restarts.
  • Internet: only for the pictures of places above, if you turn them on.
